Cancer Drugs II: Targeted Therapies

Learning objectives

  1. LO 1Trace the growth-signal chain that cancer cells hijack (receptor → RAS–RAF–MEK–ERK → cyclin D/CDK4/6 → Rb), and explain which tumor mutations matter when picking a drug.
  2. LO 2Explain how therapeutic monoclonal antibodies are produced, how to decode their names and IgG formats, and how a bound antibody gets the immune system to kill a tumor cell.
  3. LO 3For the anti-EGFR (cetuximab, panitumumab), anti-HER2 (trastuzumab, pertuzumab) and anti-VEGF (bevacizumab) antibodies: know what each binds, when it is used and which toxicity it causes.
  4. LO 4For the small-molecule kinase blockers (EGFR, BCR-ABL, ALK, BRAF, MEK and CDK4/6 inhibitors): know each target, the cancer it treats and its adverse effects.
  5. LO 5Use a tumor's molecular test results (companion diagnostics) to choose the targeted drug most likely to work.
  6. LO 6Explain how tumors escape targeted drugs and which drug pairings are designed to delay or overcome that escape.

MAPBig picture

  • Growth factor → receptor tyrosine kinase → RAS → RAF → MEK → ERK → cyclin D–CDK4/6 → Rb-P → E2F → S phase
    • Cancer turns it on by amplifying (HER2), mutating (EGFR, RAS, BRAF), fusing (BCR-ABL, EML4-ALK) or losing a brake (PTEN)
  • -mab = antibody → outside the cell → IV, long half-life → blocks ligand/receptor + recruits complement/NK cells
    • -ximab chimeric → -zumab humanized → -umab human (less mouse = less immunogenic)
  • -ib = small molecule → inside the cell → oral, short half-life → blocks the kinase pocket
  • Toxicity follows the target → EGFR = skin + gut · HER2 = heart · VEGF = BP, clots, perforation · CDK4/6 = cytopenias
  • Test first → companion diagnostic picks the drug (HER2+, EGFR del19/L858R, ALK, BRAF V600E, KRAS wild type)
  • Resistance → new target mutation · downstream activation · bypass · heterogeneity
    • Fix → re-test, switch (osimertinib), or block twice (BRAF + MEK)

LO 1Cancer signaling and tumor geneticsThe relay race from growth factor to DNA replication, and the mutations that rig it

By the end: you can draw the receptor → RAS–RAF–MEK–ERK → cyclin D/CDK4/6 → Rb/E2F chain, place every targeted drug of this lecture on it, name the five ways a tumor switches the chain on, and match KRAS, NRAS and BRAF to their cancers.
Q1. Why do "targeted" drugs care so much about signaling pathways?
  • Normal cells divide only when told to: a growth factor binds a transmembrane receptor → receptor dimerizes → its cytoplasmic tyrosine-kinase domain switches on → a chain of intracellular kinases relays the signal → gene expression changes.
    • Outputs: proliferation, survival (less apoptosis), invasion and migration.
  • Cancer cells rig this relay so it is "on" without a real signal → the rigged protein becomes a vulnerability (the tumor depends on it, the drug can hit it).
  • Tumors also talk to their stroma:
    • release angiogenesis factors (VEGF) → new blood vessels;
    • express immune checkpoint proteins (PD-L1) → switch off T cells (checkpoint drugs belong to the next session).
  • Two drug families hit these targets:

Memory tool

The relay race: the signal is a baton passed receptor → RAS → RAF → MEK → ERK. A drug only works if it grabs the runner holding the baton. If a later runner already sprints on his own (mutant KRAS/BRAF), tackling the first runner (EGFR) changes nothing.

"Real Rabbits Make Excellent Cyclists" = RAS, RAF, MEK, ERK, Cyclin D.

Step 1 HY
  • RAS = small GTPase; oncogenic mutations lock it in the GTP-bound (on) state by killing its GTPase activity.
  • PTEN = tumor suppressor phosphatase that opposes PI3K; germline loss = Cowden syndrome.
  • Rb = tumor suppressor (retinoblastoma, osteosarcoma); HPV E7 inactivates Rb, E6 degrades p53.
Q3. How does EGFR (HER1) work, and which mutations make lung cancers depend on it?
  • Extracellular part = four domains (I–IV):
    • Domains I and III bind ligand: EGF, TGF-α, amphiregulin.
    • Binding → conformational change → dimerization through domains II and IV.
  • Intracellular part = tyrosine kinase + C-terminal tail.
    • Dimer partners cross-phosphorylate → phosphorylated tail recruits substrates → RAS and PI3K pathways.
  • HER family: EGFR = HER1, HER2, HER3 → they crosstalk (heterodimerize).
  • Activating EGFR mutations in non-small cell lung cancer (in the kinase domain) → ↑ downstream signaling → growth + survival advantage → sensitivity to EGFR kinase inhibitors:
    • Exon 19 in-frame deletion (E746–A750) ≈ 45%
    • Exon 21 L858R (leucine → arginine) ≈ 40%
    • → Genotype screening of EGFR picks who gets gefitinib / erlotinib / afatinib.
CheckWhich two EGFR domains bind ligand, and which two form the dimer contact?
I and III bind ligand; II and IV dimerize. Cetuximab sits on domain III, so ligand cannot bind and the receptor never dimerizes.

Q5. How does cyclin D–CDK4/6 push a cell from G1 into S phase?
  • Rb (hypophosphorylated = active) binds the transcription factor E2F (with DP1) → E2F target genes repressed → cell held in G0/G1.
  • Cyclin D–CDK4/6 (made in response to ERK signaling) phosphorylates Rb → Rb lets go → E2F free → S-phase genes (DNA replication enzymes) on → G1 → S.
  • CDK4/6 inhibitor (palbociclib) → Rb stays unphosphorylated/active → E2F stays bound → G1 arrest.
CheckWould palbociclib work in a tumor that has completely lost Rb?
No. CDK4/6 inhibition works by keeping Rb active. With no Rb, E2F is already free, so blocking the kinase that phosphorylates Rb achieves nothing (a downstream-loss logic you will reuse in LO 6).
Step 1 trap

Phosphorylated Rb = inactive (cell cycles); unphosphorylated Rb = active brake. A question that says "CDK4/6 inhibitor → ↑ Rb phosphorylation" is wrong.

ApplyLO 1 application questions

A1A colorectal cancer carries an activating KRAS mutation. Would blocking EGFR stop proliferation?
No. Mutant KRAS signals on its own downstream of EGFR, so the relay keeps running whatever happens at the receptor.
A2Predict: a tumor with PTEN loss is treated with an EGFR antibody. Which arm keeps signaling?
The PI3K → AKT → mTOR survival arm. Without PTEN, PI3K signaling is unopposed even when receptor input falls.
A3Why does a HER2-amplified breast cancer have a worse prognosis, yet a better chance of a targeted response, than a HER2-negative one?
More receptors → more growth signaling and metastasis (worse prognosis), but the tumor depends on HER2, so trastuzumab has a target to hit.
A4A lung adenocarcinoma has an exon 19 deletion in EGFR. What does this predict?
Initial sensitivity to EGFR kinase inhibitors (gefitinib, erlotinib, afatinib). Del19 (~45%) and L858R (~40%) are the common activating, drug-sensitizing mutations.

Q4. Once bound to a tumor cell, how does the antibody get it killed?

Separate the pharmacologic effect (blocking the target) from the immune effect (recruiting killers). Bound mAbs trigger three immune mechanisms:

MechanismAntibody region → receptorEffector → result
CDC (complement-dependent cytotoxicity)C1q binds the CH2 constant domainClassical pathway → membrane-attack complex (MAC) → lysis
CDCC (complement-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity)C3b made during the cascade = opsonin → C3b receptorMacrophages / NK cells → phagocytosis, cytolysis
ADCC / ADCP (antibody-dependent cell-mediated cytotoxicity / phagocytosis)Fc region (lower hinge–CH2) → FcγRIIIa (CD16)NK cells lyse; macrophages phagocytose
CheckWhich mechanism needs no effector cell at all?
CDC. Complement alone builds the MAC pore. CDCC and ADCC both need macrophages or NK cells.
Step 1 trap
  • Some tables place the Fcγ receptor contact on CH3. Fcγ receptors actually bind the lower hinge–CH2 region; the CH2–CH3 junction binds FcRn (the recycling receptor that gives IgG its long half-life).
  • CD16 (FcγRIIIa) is the NK-cell marker for ADCC. CD56 is the NK identity marker; CD16 is the Fc receptor that does the ADCC work.
Memory tool

C-D-C = Complement Does it Completely (no cell). Add one more C (Cell) → CDCC needs a cell. ADCC = the Antibody's tail itself is the handle the NK cell grabs.

ApplyLO 2 application questions

A1A company wants an anti-PD-1 antibody that blocks the receptor without destroying the activated T cells it binds. Which IgG subclass should it choose?
IgG4. It cannot activate classical complement and has weak effector function, which is the format of nivolumab and pembrolizumab.
A2After several months of a chimeric antibody, a patient's drug levels fall and infusion reactions start. Explain, and suggest a fix.
Anti-drug antibodies against the mouse variable region. Switch to a humanized or fully human antibody against the same target (e.g., cetuximab → panitumumab).
A3NK cells from a patient lack functional CD16. Which mAb killing mechanism is lost?
ADCC. CD16 = FcγRIIIa, the receptor that binds the antibody Fc region. CDC still works.
A4How does an antibody–drug conjugate reduce off-target toxicity compared with giving the free cytotoxic drug?
The drug is delivered only to cells bearing the antigen (taken up by endocytosis and released inside), so normal cells see much less free drug.

LO 3Antibodies against EGFR, HER2 and VEGFCetuximab / panitumumab · trastuzumab / pertuzumab · bevacizumab (and ranibizumab)

By the end: you can state each antibody's target and binding site, its main cancer, its signature toxicity and the mechanism behind it, and explain why KRAS-mutant colon cancer ignores cetuximab and why trastuzumab is paired with paclitaxel rather than doxorubicin.
Q1. Where exactly do the anti-EGFR and anti-HER2 drugs bind?
  • Cetuximab: chimeric IgG1 → EGFR domain III (ligand-binding) → ligand can't bind → no dimerization → RAS kinase cascade blocked.
    • Panitumumab: fully human IgG2, same site and mechanism, fewer adverse effects.
  • Trastuzumab: humanized IgG1 → HER2 domain IV → blocks HER2 dimerization/activity and signaling.
  • Pertuzumab: humanized → HER2 domain II → blocks heterodimerization (crosstalk) of HER2 with EGFR/HER3.
    • Because HER2 crosstalks with EGFR, blocking HER2 also ↓ EGFR-driven proliferation.
  • Lapatinib / neratinib: small molecules that block the kinase of both EGFR and HER2 (LO 4).
CheckTrastuzumab and pertuzumab both bind HER2. Why can they be given together?
They bind different domains (IV vs II) and block different things (HER2 signaling vs heterodimerization), so the effects add.
Q2. When is cetuximab used, what does it do to the patient, and when is it useless?
  • Use: numerous solid tumors, e.g., metastatic colon cancer combined with FOLFIRI (folinic acid + 5-FU + irinotecan).
  • Adverse effects = mechanism-dependent: skin and gut epithelium need EGFR signaling to renew →
    • Skin: acneiform (papulopustular) rash, dermatitis.
    • GI tract: diarrhea.
    • Infusion reactions (chimeric protein).
  • Rash correlates with benefit: patients with worse rash live longer → more skin toxicity = more drug activity; little or no rash may mean too little drug.
  • Resistance: downstream activating KRAS mutation → signal runs without EGFR → cetuximab does nothing (EGFR-independent signal).
CheckA patient on FOLFIRI + cetuximab develops a strong facial acneiform rash and asks if the drug is failing. What do you say?
The rash is an expected on-target effect, and its severity correlates with better response and survival. Treat it symptomatically rather than reading it as failure.
Step 1 HY
  • Cetuximab / panitumumab: only for RAS wild-type metastatic colorectal cancer; cetuximab also for head and neck squamous cell carcinoma.
  • Anti-EGFR antibodies also cause hypomagnesemia (EGFR drives Mg²⁺ reabsorption in the distal convoluted tubule).
Step 1 trap

"Shut off the pathway at the receptor" is the wrong reflex when the tumor has a KRAS or BRAF activating mutation. Upstream blockers are useless when the driver sits downstream.

Q3. How well does anti-HER2 therapy work, and why does it hurt the heart?
  • Works in the ~20% of breast cancers that are HER2-positive (by IHC protein overexpression and/or FISH gene amplification).
  • Adding trastuzumab to chemotherapy restores the outcome of HER2+ cancers to that of HER2− cancers.
  • Used in combination (e.g., with paclitaxel).
  • Pertuzumab + trastuzumab (+ docetaxel) in metastatic HER2+ disease: median overall survival 56.5 vs 40.8 months = +15.7 months.

Q4. How does bevacizumab work if it barely kills tumor cells, and what does it do to the patient?
  • Bevacizumab: humanized IgG1 that binds the ligand VEGF (not the receptor) → VEGF can't reach VEGFR on endothelium → angiogenesis inhibited.
  • Why tumors make VEGF: hypoxia → VEGF → new vessels that are leaky.
  • Alone it is a weak anticancer drug. Its value is in combination:
    • Normalizes tumor vasculature → less leaky → ↓ interstitial pressure → better delivery of cytotoxic drugs.
    • Better immune-cell access and recognition → combined with checkpoint inhibitors.
    • IFL (leucovorin + 5-FU + irinotecan, a FOLFIRI-type regimen) + bevacizumab in colon cancer: median survival 20.3 vs 15.6 months.
  • Adverse effects: hypertension, thromboembolism, GI perforation.
    • Hypertension: VEGF normally promotes NO-mediated vasodilation → blocking it ↑ vascular tone.
  • Ranibizumab: bevacizumab-related humanized Fab fragment (no Fc, altered antigen-binding sequence) → intravitreal injection for wet (neovascular) macular degeneration → markedly improves vision.
CheckOn bevacizumab + chemotherapy, a patient develops sudden severe abdominal pain with free air under the diaphragm. What happened?
GI perforation, a signature anti-VEGF toxicity (with hypertension and thromboembolism).
Step 1 HY
  • Bevacizumab also causes impaired wound healing, hemorrhage and proteinuria → hold it around surgery.
  • Uses: colorectal, NSCLC, renal cell carcinoma, glioblastoma; intravitreal anti-VEGF (ranibizumab, bevacizumab, aflibercept) for wet AMD and diabetic macular edema.
Step 1 trap

Bevacizumab binds the ligand (VEGF-A). Small molecules ending in -anib (sunitinib, sorafenib, pazopanib) block the VEGF receptor kinase inside the cell.

ApplyLO 3 application questions

A1A metastatic colon cancer is being considered for cetuximab. Sequencing shows an activating KRAS mutation. Will it work?
No. KRAS signals downstream of EGFR, independent of the receptor. KRAS testing is the companion test for anti-EGFR antibodies.
A2Three months into trastuzumab, a patient has dyspnea and an ejection fraction drop from 60% to 40%. Mechanism and likely course?
Loss of HER2 survival/repair signaling in cardiomyocytes; usually reversible after stopping. Different from doxorubicin's oxidative, irreversible injury.
A3A 74-year-old has sudden distortion of central vision from a subretinal neovascular membrane. Which relative of an anticancer antibody is injected into the eye?
Ranibizumab, an anti-VEGF humanized Fab fragment, given intravitreally for wet macular degeneration.
A4A patient is scheduled for bowel resection while on bevacizumab. What is the concern?
Impaired wound healing, bleeding and perforation, because anti-VEGF blocks the new-vessel growth that healing needs. Hold the drug around surgery.

Q2. How are EGFR kinase inhibitors chosen, and what is T790M?
  • Gefitinib, erlotinib, afatinib = EGFR tyrosine-kinase inhibitors (TKIs) → block the intracellular kinase downstream of HER1.
    • Use: non-small cell lung cancer with activating EGFR mutations (del19, L858R).
    • EGFR kinase inhibitors are designed and selected based on the mutations in EGFR.
  • T790M = threonine → methionine at codon 790 of the kinase domain → found in ~50% of tumors that become resistant to gefitinib/erlotinib/afatinib.
    • → Osimertinib was designed to inhibit T790M-mutant EGFR.
    • Osimertinib after chemoradiotherapy in EGFR-mutant NSCLC markedly prolongs progression-free survival.
CheckA lung cancer responded to gefitinib for 10 months and now progresses; rebiopsy shows T790M. Next drug?
Osimertinib. It inhibits T790M-mutant EGFR.
Q3. Why do EGFR inhibitors cause a rash, and why does osimertinib cause less?
  • Adverse effects of EGFR TKIs mirror cetuximab: skin and GI tract (rash, diarrhea).
  • Mechanism: EGFR drives renewal of basal-layer keratinocytes → blockade → growth arrest and premature differentiation of basal keratinocytes → follicular inflammation → papulopustular rash.
    • Applies to all EGFR inhibitors (cetuximab, panitumumab, gefitinib, erlotinib).
  • Pattern (most to least common): papulopustular rash on face and upper trunk → hair changes (scalp, eyelashes) → xerosis / pruritus → periungual inflammation.
  • Skin toxicity correlates with anticancer effect (Q2 of LO 3).
  • Osimertinib preferentially inhibits mutant EGFR (incl. T790M) and largely spares wild-type EGFR in normal skin → fewer skin adverse effects.
CheckWhich cell type is the "victim" behind the EGFR-inhibitor rash, and what happens to it?
Basal-layer keratinocytes undergo growth arrest and premature differentiation.
Step 1 HY

Erlotinib / gefitinib / afatinib / osimertinib → EGFR-mutant NSCLC (classic: nonsmoking woman with adenocarcinoma). AEs: rash, diarrhea; osimertinib also QT prolongation.

Step 1 trap
  • T790M is a resistance mutation, not a sensitizing one. Del19 / L858R = sensitizing → first-generation TKIs; T790M = acquired resistance → osimertinib.
  • Osimertinib has less skin toxicity because it spares wild-type EGFR; it is not the classic rash drug.
Q4. What does imatinib block, and why can it lower the white count?
  • Philadelphia chromosome t(9;22) → BCR-ABL fusion = constitutively active tyrosine kinase → phosphorylates many substrates → ↑ mitogenic signaling and genomic instability, ↓ apoptosis → chronic myeloid leukemia (CML).
  • Imatinib blocks the BCR-ABL kinase; it also inhibits PDGFR (PDGF signaling) and c-KIT.
  • Adverse effect: leukopenia (myelosuppression) → imatinib also blocks normal kinase signaling in hematopoietic progenitors (c-KIT, PDGFR) and suppresses the leukemic clone that is producing most blood cells.
  • Lapatinib (neratinib similar): small-molecule inhibitor of both EGFR and HER2 kinases → HER2+ breast cancer.
CheckBesides BCR-ABL, which growth-factor receptor signaling does imatinib inhibit?
PDGF receptor (and c-KIT).
Step 1 HY
  • Imatinib (dasatinib, nilotinib): CML, Ph+ ALL, GIST (c-KIT mutation).
  • AEs: myelosuppression, fluid retention / periorbital edema, muscle cramps/myalgia, ↑ LFTs.
  • Resistance in CML: BCR-ABL kinase mutations (e.g., T315I "gatekeeper") → ponatinib.
Q5. Which inhibitors target ALK, BRAF and MEK, and why are BRAF and MEK blockers given together?
  • Alectinib, crizotinib → EML4-ALK fusion kinase (ALK rearrangement) in non-small cell lung cancer; alectinib (newer) gives better progression-free survival.
    • EML4-ALK can also be the bypass route that makes a tumor resistant to EGFR inhibitors (LO 6).
  • Vemurafenib (dabrafenib similar) → V600E-mutant BRAF → partial or complete regression of metastatic melanoma (mutant BRAF = major melanoma driver).
    • Single agent → resistance and relapse within ~½ year.
  • Trametinib → MEK inhibitor → blocks the same pathway one step further down.
    • BRAF + MEK inhibition beats BRAF alone in advanced melanoma and delays resistance.
    • Adjuvant dabrafenib + trametinib vs placebo: 5-year relapse-free survival ~52% vs ~36%.
    • Rule: to prevent resistance to a kinase inhibitor, add one that acts further downstream.
CheckWhich drug is usually added to vemurafenib, and what does it inhibit?
Trametinib, a MEK inhibitor (second hit, further downstream in the same pathway).
Step 1 HY
  • BRAF inhibitor alone → paradoxical MAPK activation in BRAF-wild-type keratinocytes → cutaneous squamous cell carcinomas / keratoacanthomas; adding a MEK inhibitor reduces them.
  • Other BRAF-inhibitor AEs: rash, photosensitivity, fatigue, arthralgia; ALK inhibitors: edema, vision changes (crizotinib), GI upset.
Buzzwords
"EML4-ALK" → alectinib / crizotinib"V600E melanoma" → vemurafenib/dabrafenib + trametinib"t(9;22), CML" → imatinib"T790M" → osimertinib
Q6. What does palbociclib do, who gets it, and why so many side effects?
  • Palbociclib (-ciclib: also ribociclib, abemaciclib) → inhibits CDK4/6 → Rb stays unphosphorylated/active → E2F repressed → G1 arrest (Rb–E2F figure in LO 1).
  • Use: metastatic, HER2-negative (!), ER-positive breast cancer combined with an anti-hormone such as letrozole (aromatase inhibitor → depletes estrogen) → moderate gain in progression-free survival.
  • Adverse effects: cytopenia, nausea, stomatitis, alopecia, upper respiratory infections, fatigue, peripheral neuropathy → it blocks the cell cycle in normal proliferating cells too.
CheckWhy would palbociclib not be paired with trastuzumab for its standard indication?
Its standard indication is HER2-negative (ER+) breast cancer, which has no HER2 target for trastuzumab.
Step 1 trap

Palbociclib is for ER+ / HER2− breast cancer, partnered with an aromatase inhibitor or fulvestrant. Don't pick it for triple-negative disease, and don't pair it with trastuzumab for the classic indication. Boards list neutropenia/myelosuppression (and pneumonitis) as its main toxicity.

LO 5Choosing the drug from the tumor's molecular profileCompanion diagnostics: test first, then treat

By the end: you can define a companion diagnostic, tell a prognostic marker from a predictive one, name the test result that unlocks each targeted drug, and explain what the Oncotype test adds.
Q1. What is a companion diagnostic and why bother?
  • Companion diagnostic = a test on the cancer specimen that selects patients with a higher chance of responding to a particular drug and avoids treating cancers that are resistant from the start (ab initio resistance).
  • What is measured:
    • Gene and protein expression in the tumor sample (e.g., HER2 by IHC/FISH, estrogen receptor).
    • Mutations (EGFR, KRAS, BRAF) and rearrangements (ALK).
    • Shed cancer material in the blood, e.g., circulating tumor DNA ("liquid biopsy") → also used to follow the cancer over time.
  • Altered pathways give two kinds of information:
    • Prognostic → predicts outcome (how aggressive).
    • Predictive → guides the choice of treatment (will this drug work).
  • Every companion diagnostic requires clinical evidence that it actually separates responders from non-responders.
  • Logic link: amplification or activating mutation of the drug's own target (HER2 amplification, EGFR del19) predicts a response to the drug aimed at that target, because the tumor depends on it.
CheckHER2 amplification: prognostic, predictive, or both?
Both. It predicts worse outcome without treatment (prognostic) and response to trastuzumab (predictive).
Step 1 trap
  • Amplification of a driver target (HER2) predicts response. Amplification of a drug's enzyme target in a cytotoxic setting (e.g., DHFR amplification with methotrexate) is a classic resistance mechanism: more enzyme soaks up the drug. Read which situation the stem describes.
  • A wild-type result can be the "go" signal: anti-EGFR antibodies need RAS wild-type colorectal cancer.

Q3. What does the Oncotype test measure?
  • Measures mRNA expression of a panel of genes in an (ER+) breast cancer specimen → combined into a recurrence score.
  • Risk of distant recurrence rises continuously with the score → identifies which patients actually benefit from chemotherapy (high score) and which can safely skip it (low score).
  • It is a gene-expression (multi-gene) test, not a single-mutation test.
CheckA woman with early ER+/HER2− breast cancer has a low recurrence score. What does it suggest?
Low risk of distant recurrence and little benefit from adding chemotherapy; endocrine therapy is the mainstay.

ApplyLO 5 application questions

A1A 45-year-old with metastatic melanoma has a BRAF V600E mutation. Which targeted regimen gives the most durable control?
BRAF inhibitor (dabrafenib or vemurafenib) + MEK inhibitor (trametinib). BRAF alone relapses in about 6 months.
A2A postmenopausal woman has ER+/HER2− metastatic breast cancer. Which targeted combination fits, and which antibody would NOT help?
Palbociclib + letrozole; trastuzumab would not help because the tumor is HER2-negative.
A3Before giving cetuximab for metastatic colon cancer, which result do you need?
RAS (KRAS/NRAS) wild type (and ideally BRAF wild type). A downstream activating mutation makes EGFR blockade useless.
A4A patient cannot undergo another lung biopsy at progression. How can T790M still be detected?
Circulating tumor DNA in plasma (liquid biopsy), using shed tumor DNA to genotype the resistance.

  • BCL-2 = anti-apoptotic protein (blocks mitochondrial apoptosis) → overexpression/mutation lets tumor cells survive drug-induced damage → resistance via inactivated apoptosis. It sits on the PI3K–AKT survival arm of the pathway map (LO 1).

Inactivation of apoptosis signals. Damaged cells survive instead of dying.

  • t(14;18) places BCL-2 under the IgH promoter → follicular lymphoma. Venetoclax inhibits BCL-2 (CLL, AML).
  • P-glycoprotein (MDR1 / ABCB1) = ATP-dependent efflux pump → multidrug resistance to many cytotoxic drugs (anthracyclines, vinca alkaloids, taxanes).

Q2. How do tumors escape EGFR blockade, and why does single-agent BRAF inhibition fail?

EGFR inhibitors (TKIs and cetuximab) fail through three routes

  1. New EGFR mutation → T790M (~50% of TKI-resistant lung cancers) → switch to osimertinib.
  2. Downstream activating mutation → mutant KRAS signals independently of EGFR (same reason cetuximab fails in KRAS-mutant colon cancer).
  3. Bypass pathway → e.g., EML4-ALK translocation → treat with alectinib / crizotinib.

Single-agent BRAF inhibition fails through heterogeneity

  • Nearly every melanoma cell carries BRAF V600E, yet tumors return within months: different metastases follow branched evolution and acquire different escape alterations that reactivate MAPK or PI3K–AKT signaling (RAS mutation, BRAF amplification or splice variants, MEK mutation, PI3K–AKT activation).
  • → The drug selects resistant clones.
CheckA colorectal cancer has an activating BRAF mutation. A resident proposes cetuximab "to shut off the pathway at the receptor." What is wrong?
The driver sits downstream of EGFR, so the pathway runs whatever happens at the receptor. Blocking upstream is useless.
Memory tool

The 3 escape doors from EGFR blockade = "Change the lock, go around, or go below": change the lock (T790M) · go around (ALK bypass) · go below (KRAS).

Step 1 trap

When a targeted drug stops working, the answer is rarely "increase the dose." The tested move is re-biopsy / ctDNA → identify the mechanism → switch (T790M → osimertinib; ALK → alectinib) or add a downstream inhibitor (MEK).

1A 61-year-old man has metastatic colorectal adenocarcinoma. Tumor sequencing shows an activating mutation at codon 12 of KRAS. The oncology team is choosing between several agents. Which drug is least likely to provide benefit?

C. Cetuximab. Mutant KRAS signals downstream of EGFR, independent of the receptor, so blocking EGFR (cetuximab, panitumumab) is useless. 5-FU, irinotecan and oxaliplatin are cytotoxic backbones that do not depend on RAS status. Bevacizumab targets VEGF in the stroma and is used regardless of KRAS.

2A 52-year-old woman with HER2-amplified breast cancer is receiving adjuvant trastuzumab. Four months later she reports exertional dyspnea; echocardiography shows the ejection fraction has fallen from 62% to 41%. Endomyocardial biopsy would be expected to show minimal myocyte necrosis. Which mechanism best explains her cardiac dysfunction?

A. HER2 (ErbB2) supports cardiomyocyte survival/repair; blocking it reduces contractility, often reversibly and without much necrosis. B is doxorubicin (oxidative stress, iron), which is why the two drugs are not combined. C fits checkpoint inhibitors. D is bevacizumab. E describes taxanes/vinca alkaloids, not cardiotoxicity of trastuzumab.

3A 48-year-old man with fatigue and splenomegaly has a WBC of 140,000/µL with a full spectrum of maturing granulocytes. Cytogenetics show t(9;22). He starts an oral tyrosine-kinase inhibitor and two months later his WBC is 2,300/µL. Besides the fusion kinase, the drug also inhibits which receptor?

B. PDGF receptor. Imatinib inhibits BCR-ABL, PDGFR and c-KIT; blocking normal kinase signaling in progenitors contributes to leukopenia. EGFR is the target of gefitinib/cetuximab, VEGF-A of bevacizumab (an antibody, not oral), MEK of trametinib and CDK4/6 of palbociclib.

4A 58-year-old woman who never smoked has metastatic lung adenocarcinoma with an EGFR exon 19 deletion. She responds well to gefitinib for 11 months, then new liver lesions appear. Plasma circulating tumor DNA shows a threonine-to-methionine substitution at codon 790 of EGFR. Which is the most appropriate next agent?

D. Osimertinib was designed to inhibit T790M-mutant EGFR (found in ~50% of resistant tumors). Erlotinib is a first-generation TKI with the same resistance problem. Cetuximab targets the EGFR ectodomain and does not overcome kinase-domain resistance in lung cancer. Imatinib targets BCR-ABL; trastuzumab targets HER2.

5A 39-year-old man with BRAF V600E metastatic melanoma has dramatic shrinkage of dozens of subcutaneous nodules after 15 weeks of single-agent vemurafenib, but the nodules regrow by week 23. Biopsies of different nodules reveal different alterations reactivating the MAPK pathway. Adding which agent at the start would most likely have delayed this?

E. Trametinib, a MEK inhibitor, blocks the same pathway further downstream (vertical inhibition); BRAF + MEK delays resistance and improves survival. The other options act on unrelated targets (VEGF, EGFR, BCR-ABL, CDK4/6) and do not address MAPK reactivation.

6A 67-year-old woman has metastatic breast cancer that is estrogen-receptor positive and HER2 negative. She is started on letrozole plus an oral agent that causes neutropenia, fatigue and stomatitis. What is the direct molecular effect of the oral agent?

A. Palbociclib inhibits CDK4/6, so Rb stays unphosphorylated, E2F stays bound and cells arrest in G1; normal dividing cells are hit too (cytopenias). B is pertuzumab (irrelevant in HER2− disease). C is letrozole itself. D is paclitaxel. E is imatinib.

7A 63-year-old man receives FOLFIRI plus a humanized IgG1 antibody for metastatic colon cancer. Over 6 weeks his blood pressure rises from 128/80 to 168/98 mm Hg, and he later presents with acute abdominal pain and free air under the diaphragm. What does the antibody bind?

B. Bevacizumab binds the VEGF ligand, causing hypertension, thromboembolism and GI perforation. D describes small-molecule -anib drugs (sunitinib, pazopanib) acting inside the cell, not an antibody. A = cetuximab (rash, diarrhea). C = trastuzumab (cardiotoxicity). E = nivolumab/pembrolizumab (immune-related adverse events).

8A pharmacology student reads that a new antibody ends in "-zumab." Which description of its structure is most accurate?

D. -zumab = humanized: mouse CDRs grafted into a human IgG. B is -ximab (chimeric, e.g., cetuximab). C is -umab (e.g., panitumumab). A is -omab (old murine antibodies). E describes a bispecific such as blinatumomab.

9A biotech team is designing an antibody against a tumor-surface antigen. Their goal is maximal tumor-cell lysis through the classical complement pathway as well as strong Fcγ-receptor engagement. Which format should they choose?

E. IgG1 (with IgG3) activates classical complement and binds Fcγ receptors most potently, which is why most tumor-killing mAbs are IgG1. IgG4 cannot activate the classical pathway (used for anti-PD-1). IgG2 is weaker. A Fab fragment (like ranibizumab) has no Fc at all. IgA does not activate the classical pathway.

10Three weeks after starting erlotinib for EGFR-mutant lung cancer, a 55-year-old woman develops an acneiform, papulopustular eruption on her face and upper chest with dry, itchy skin on her arms. Which mechanism underlies this reaction?

B. EGFR drives basal keratinocyte renewal; EGFR inhibitors cause growth arrest and premature differentiation → follicular inflammation. The rash correlates with drug activity. C is the BRAF-inhibitor mechanism for cutaneous SCC/keratoacanthomas. A, D and E are unrelated hypersensitivity, pemphigus and phototoxic mechanisms.

11A 44-year-old nonsmoking woman has stage IV lung adenocarcinoma. Molecular testing shows EGFR wild type, KRAS wild type and an EML4-ALK rearrangement. Which is the most appropriate targeted therapy?

C. Alectinib (or crizotinib) inhibits the EML4-ALK fusion kinase; alectinib gives better progression-free survival. Gefitinib needs an EGFR-activating mutation. Cetuximab is not a lung-cancer companion-test drug. Vemurafenib needs BRAF V600E; trastuzumab needs HER2 amplification.

12Tumor cells coated with an IgG1 anticancer antibody are mixed with natural killer cells, and the tumor cells are lysed even when complement is heat-inactivated. Which receptor on the NK cells is most directly responsible?

A. ADCC: the antibody Fc region (lower hinge–CH2) binds FcγRIIIa (CD16) on NK cells, which then lyse the target. The C3b receptor (B) mediates CDCC and needs complement, which was inactivated. C1q (D) starts CDC, also complement-dependent. FcRn (C) recycles IgG and sets its half-life. CD3 (E) is the T-cell receptor complex used by bispecifics such as blinatumomab.

13A 57-year-old man has a slowly progressive lymphoma composed of small cleaved cells in nodular aggregates. Cytogenetics show t(14;18). His tumor responds poorly to several DNA-damaging drugs. Overexpression of which protein most likely explains the drug resistance?

C. BCL-2. t(14;18) puts BCL-2 under the IgH promoter (follicular lymphoma); this anti-apoptotic protein lets cells survive drug-induced damage (resistance by inactivating apoptosis). P-glycoprotein is efflux-based resistance, not linked to t(14;18). Thymidylate synthase overexpression causes 5-FU resistance. T790M is an EGFR-TKI resistance mutation in lung cancer. Cyclin D1 is t(11;14), mantle cell lymphoma.
